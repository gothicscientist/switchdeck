How Thock works

Thock is a free custom mechanical keyboard finder and builder, like a PC parts picker for keyboards. You answer eight questions, and it puts together complete builds from parts that real stores sell today, with prices, stock and a link for every part.

The finder

The finder asks one question at a time, and the field of keyboards behind it changes with each answer:

  1. Size: 60%, 65%, 75%, tenkeyless or full size.
  2. Feel: linear, tactile, clicky or cushioned (silent) switches.
  3. Sound: deep thock, creamy, crisp clack or quiet, with a real typing recording for each.
  4. Look: dark, light, colorful or retro keycaps, or a surprise.
  5. Use: gaming, work and typing, or both.
  6. Connection: wired or wireless (Bluetooth or 2.4 GHz).
  7. Soldering: hot-swap only, or open to soldered PCBs.
  8. Budget: a total for the whole keyboard.

Thock scores every switch and keycap set in the catalog against your answers, then assembles builds best first and avoids repeating the same parts, so different tastes get different keyboards. Each result shows a match score, the expected sound and feel (pitch, volume, bounce and key weight), every part with its store links, and up to three suggested sound mods such as case foam, PE foam or switch films that fit your remaining budget. You can swap any part and the build is rescored.

The builder

The builder is for picking parts yourself: a case or kit, PCB, plate, switches, stabilizers, keycaps and extras. It draws the build as you go, totals the price and checks compatibility:

  • Switches must match the board's switch family: MX-style mechanical, magnetic Hall effect, low-profile or optical.
  • The PCB and plate must be made for the case, and the keycap set must cover its layout.
  • Stabilizers must suit the PCB (clip-in or screw-in) or the plate, and 5-pin switches on a 3-pin PCB are flagged.
  • It tells you when a PCB needs soldering and when switches press in by hand (hot-swap).

Thock only says a part fits when the store's own data confirms it. When a spec isn't published, it says so and asks you to check instead of guessing. Builds can be saved in your browser and shared as a link that restores the exact build.

Thock is independent and not affiliated with any store or brand. Some store links are affiliate links (including Amazon, Keychron and Epomaker), which may earn Thock a commission at no extra cost to you. Affiliate links never change which parts are recommended.

Keyboard terms

Thock
A deep, rounded, low-pitched typing sound, usually from heavier or longer-pole switches, thick PBT keycaps and a damped case. The site is named after it.
Clack
A brighter, sharper, higher-pitched sound, common with ABS keycaps and stiffer plates.
Linear switch
Smooth and even from top to bottom, with no bump. Popular for gaming.
Tactile switch
A bump partway down the press that you can feel, without a click.
Clicky switch
A tactile bump plus an audible click. Loud in shared rooms.
Hot-swap
A PCB with sockets, so switches press in and pull out by hand with no soldering.
Hall effect
Magnetic switches whose actuation point can be adjusted in software. They need a Hall effect PCB.
60%, 65%, 75%
Compact layouts: 60% drops the arrows and function row, 65% adds arrows, 75% adds a function row.
TKL
Tenkeyless: a full layout without the number pad.
Gasket mount
The plate sits on soft gaskets instead of screws, for a softer, more even typing feel.
